# What to Eat in Bahrain — The Complete Culinary Guide 2026

Bahrain's food scene is a reflection of its geography and history — a Gulf island nation with deep Bedouin traditions, influences from Persia, East Africa, and the Indian subcontinent, and a modern cosmopolitan palate. The result is one of the most diverse and delicious food cultures in the Arabian Gulf.

Whether you're a visitor experiencing Bahraini cuisine for the first time or a resident looking to explore beyond the familiar, this guide covers the dishes you need to try and where to find them.

## Bahrain's National Dishes

### Machboos

Machboos is Bahrain's national dish — a spiced rice dish cooked with meat (usually chicken, lamb, or fish) in a broth flavoured with a blend of dried lime, cinnamon, cardamom, and cumin. The rice is cooked until it absorbs all the broth, giving it a distinctive colour and depth of flavour.

**Where to try:** Most traditional Bahraini restaurants serve machboos. For an authentic experience, try the Machboos at **Darseen** in Muharraq or **Abu Nahda** restaurant. High-end versions are available at **Nama** in Amwaj Islands and **Teitei** in Juffair.

**What to expect:** A generous portion of spiced rice topped with slow-cooked meat, served with a fresh tomato-based sauce (tomoor) and pickled vegetables (khubz naaim).

### Harees

Harees is a centuries-old dish of wheat and meat cooked together until they merge into a smooth, porridgelike consistency. It's particularly associated with Ramadan and special occasions, but available year-round at traditional restaurants.

**Where to try:** Traditional Bahraini restaurants. **Al Arz Bakery** in Manama serves excellent harees, as do most establishments in the Muharraq Souq area.

### Balaleet

A sweet-savoury breakfast dish of sweetened vermicelli noodles topped with an omelette. It's a uniquely Bahraini and Gulf morning favourite, found at traditional coffee shops and Bahraini restaurants.

**Where to try:** Traditional Arabic breakfast spots and coffee shops. **Baba Ha** in Amwaj and locations across Muharraq serve balaleet as part of their breakfast menu.

## Street Food and Casual Eats

### Shawarma

The ubiquitous Middle Eastern wrap has found a beloved home in Bahrain. Bahrain's shawarma shops are known for their generous portions, distinctive spice blends, and variety of sauces.

**Where to try:** 
- **Al Majeed** in Manama — A local institution known for its chicken shawarma.
- **Shawarma Al-Muharraq** — Widely considered one of the best in Bahrain.
- Most areas have a local favourite; ask a resident and you'll get a different answer each time.

**What to order:** Chicken shawarma with garlic sauce, or mixed shawarma with both chicken and tawook.

### Falafel and Foul

A classic Arabic breakfast combination — crispy falafel made from ground fava beans or chickpeas, served with foul (slow-cooked fava beans mashed with olive oil, lemon, and garlic). Found at virtually every Arabic restaurant and most coffee shops.

**Where to try:** Most Arabic restaurants. For something more upscale, try **Le Jardin's** breakfast menu in Adliya.

### Sambosa

Bahrain's version of the samosa — a crispy triangular pastry filled with spiced meat, vegetables, or cheese. Sold at most Bahraini restaurants, coffee shops, and bakeries. A popular snack at any time of day.

## Sweet Treats

### Kunafa

Perhaps the Gulf's most famous dessert, kunafa consists of shredded phyllo dough (kataifi) soaked in sugar syrup, layered with cheese or cream, and topped with crushed pistachios. Served warm, it's a must-try.

**Where to try:**
- **Kunafa of Muharraq** — This unassuming shop in Muharraq is considered one of the best kunafa spots in the Gulf. Be prepared to queue.
- **Al Amari** in Manama — Another local favourite with a long history.
- **Hotel rooftop restaurants** during Ramadan serve excellent kunafa as part of their Iftar menus.

**Tip:** Kunafa is best eaten fresh and warm. Ask when it was made.

### Luqaymat

Golden, crispy-on-the-outside, soft-on-the-inside deep-fried dough balls rolled in sugar syrup. A traditional Bahraini and Gulf sweet found at most Arabic bakeries. Perfect with Arabic coffee.

**Where to try:** Bakeries in Muharraq and Manama. **Al Arz Bakery** is a reliable source.

### Sago (Sagu)

A traditional pearl sago pudding flavoured with rose water and served with pistachios. A heritage dessert that's becoming rarer to find in its authentic form.

**Where to try:** Traditional Bahraini restaurants and heritage-focused establishments in Muharraq.

## International Cuisines

Bahrain's expatriate population has created demand for virtually every global cuisine. Some of the standout options:

### Indian

Bahrain has a large and established Indian community, making authentic Indian food plentiful and excellent value.

- **Taj Mahal** (Manama) — North Indian cuisine with a longstanding reputation.
- **Satyam** (Adliya) — South Indian vegetarian favourites.
- The **Indian School Area** in Manama has dozens of small, authentic Indian restaurants at very reasonable prices.

### Filipino

Bahrain's Filipino community has brought authentic Filipino cuisine to the island.

- **Jeanie's** in Adliya — Popular for Filipino favourites like adobo, sinigang, and lechon.
- **Rodney's** in Juffair — Another established Filipino favourite.

### Iranian

Given Bahrain's proximity to Iran, Iranian restaurants are plentiful and excellent.

- **Shiraz** in Muharraq — Traditional Iranian cuisine with exceptional kabab koobideh.
- **Pars Restaurant** in Juffair — Known for its chelo kabab and tahdig.

### Italian

- **Café Jelly** (Amwaj) — Casual Italian with a popular brunch.
- **Arabesque** at The Bahrain Bay — Upscale Italian dining with waterfront views.
- **Arredo** in Juffair — A local favourite for wood-fired pizza and pasta.

### Japanese and Asian Fusion

- **Fusions** at The Art Hotel, Amwaj — Asian fusion with lagoon views.
- **Impossible** in Juffair — Creative plant-based Asian cuisine.
- **Nama** in Amwaj — Contemporary Pan-Asian options.

## Dining Customs and Tips

**Timing:** Lunch is typically 12pm–3pm, dinner from 7pm onwards. Many restaurants close between meals.

**Reservations:** Recommended for weekend dinners at popular restaurants, especially in Amwaj and Adliya. Call ahead or book via WhatsApp where available.

**Tipping:** 10–15% if service charge isn't included. Check your bill — many restaurants add it automatically.

**Ramadan:** Many restaurants modify their hours during Ramadan. Check social media before visiting.

**Dress code:** Casual for most places. Smart casual for upscale restaurants. Beachwear is for beach clubs only.

**Halal:** Virtually all restaurants in Bahrain are halal. Pork is not available. Alcohol is served in hotel restaurants and licensed venues only.
